What did the teacher actually see?

On a learning platform, pupils answer questions all term and teachers see a score for each pupil and topic. Some answers reach the warehouse late, after a dashboard has already shown a score. So “what was this pupil’s score last term?” has two honest answers. This project keeps both, on synthetic data.

Type
Independent project
Data
Synthetic, seeded
Scale
33,284 answers, 300 pupils
Focus
Point-in-time correctness

01The problem

The generator creates 33,284 question responses for 300 pupils across 5 topics in one term, with the faults a real feed has: 652 responses arrive twice from retried deliveries, about 4% of answers arrive 2 to 12 days late from offline devices (some after term has ended), and 12 answers carry a timestamp in the future.

The hard parts are the right grain, point-in-time correctness (“reconstruct a pupil’s score as it stood last term”), builds that give the same answer every time, and catching data quality problems before they reach a teacher.

02What I built

  • A staging model with one row per response. Retries collapse to the first load with a deterministic tie-break. Clock-skewed rows are quarantined with a reason, not dropped.
  • A type-2 score history on two clocks. valid_from and valid_to say when a score was true in the world (answer time); known_from says when the warehouse could first have shown it (load time).
  • Term-end scores answered two ways: restated, with everything known now, and as reported, what a dashboard could have shown on the last day of term.
  • Five checks on every run: one row per response, no overlapping validity windows, exactly one current row per pupil and topic, every score between 0 and 1, and known_from never earlier than valid_from.
  • Five tests, including that re-running every model gives a byte-identical result (an order-independent SHA-256 over all outputs) and that the point-in-time lookup matches a direct recomputation from raw answers.
  • Written for BigQuery and dbt: the history is built from an incremental model rather than a dbt snapshot, because snapshots capture load time and this needs answer time as well.

The late answers are planted, so the point is not that they exist. The point is that the model keeps the restated scores apart from what teachers originally saw.

A report of what teachers were told needs the as-reported view. A model trained on true ability needs the restated one. One table that silently mixes them is how a predicted grade drifts from what a teacher remembers seeing.
