Who owns whom: where the global register of legal entities contradicts itself

Banks, regulators and data vendors rely on GLEIF’s Legal Entity Identifier register to know which company owns which. I wanted to know whether the register agrees with itself.

Type
Independent project
Data
GLEIF golden copy
Runs
Weekly, published
Focus
Reference data controls

01The problem

Ownership data feeds credit exposure, sanctions screening and regulatory reporting. If a subsidiary and its parent disagree about who owns whom, every system downstream inherits the contradiction, and nobody notices until an exposure is wrong.

02What I built

  • Streamed GLEIF’s full golden copy (about 570 MB zipped, 5 GB as CSV) straight into compressed Parquet, so the CSV never touches disk.
  • 11 SQL controls over 3,478,835 entities and 492,253 parent relationships, running in about ten seconds in DuckDB.
  • Each control has an owner, a severity, the cost of a failure and a triage runbook, and returns a denominator, so every exception rate is honest.
  • Tests plant one defect per control in a synthetic register and prove each control finds its own defect and nothing else. A weekly job refreshes the data and republishes the write-up.

03The result

3,478,835
entities checked in about ten seconds
1,477
ultimate parents contradicted by the ownership chain
307
“ultimate” parents that report a parent of their own
748
of the contradictions involve a current record

RSA Insurance Group, bought by Intact Financial in 2021, still reports no known parent in a record updated on 26 October 2026, while its subsidiaries name Intact as their ultimate parent.

Two ownership loops, five entities named as their own parent and 23 duplicate registrations. The identifiers themselves were clean: all 256 that fail their check digits were already retired.

A finding means two records disagree, not which one is wrong. Settling that takes the companies’ own filings.
